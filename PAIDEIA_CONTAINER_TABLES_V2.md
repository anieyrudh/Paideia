## §0 — Quality Bar and Conventions

### 0.1 What "higher than PhET" means here

The PhET tradition is the right floor (research-based pedagogy, immediate cause-and-effect feedback, invisible-made-visible). Above that floor, every Paideia simulation should add at least three of:

- **Scenario-driven artifact, not numeric readouts.** PID = car on a track. Markov = animated agent walking the state graph. Bayes = a 1000-person population grid that recolors. Never sliders feeding a number panel.
- **Cinematic motion + typography.** Smooth tweens (Framer Motion or equivalent), readable type at a glance, generous whitespace. The sim should feel like a polished product, not a teaching applet.
- **Game-like framing where natural.** A challenge, a level, a tunable budget, a "beat the preset" goal. Tuning PID becomes a lap time; sizing a beam becomes a weight-vs-safety puzzle. Optional, never mandatory.
- **Real-data hooks where the concept supports it.** Climate sim pulls live NOAA / Copernicus data. Markov example uses real Singapore weather transitions. Newsvendor uses actual hawker-stall demand profiles.
- **AR-friendly mode where viable.** Plate tectonics, structural load paths, formal-analysis zoomable artwork — these benefit from WebXR or device-camera overlays; an explicit "place in your room" mode.
- **AI tutor probes inline.** A Socratic side-panel that watches what the learner is doing and asks one question at the right moment ("Why did Kp = 0.3 settle so slowly?"). Not a chatbot — a tightly-prompted nudger.
- **Sound design (muted by default).** Subtle physical sounds (the car engine in PID, the rain on the climate sim) — unmuted on first interaction, mute toggle obvious. Never music, never voiceover unless requested.
- **Mobile-first interactions.** Touch-drag, pinch-zoom, single-thumb operation where possible. Most students will open this on a phone first.

### 0.2 Media-column prompt conventions

Each Media cell contains four prompt slots. Use whichever apply to the container.

- **THUMB** — image-gen prompt for the catalogue thumbnail (target: 400×300 SVG-style or flat illustration; never a screenshot of UI). Tool-agnostic; works for DALL-E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, Imagen.
- **FALLBACK** — image-gen prompt for the static fallback diagram shown when the sim cannot run (target: 800×600, more annotation than thumbnail, labels in plain English).
- **INTRO** — video-gen prompt for a 5-8 second loop shown at the top of the concept card (target: silent, autoplay, looping; works for Sora, Runway Gen-3, Veo, Pika).
- **3D ASSETS** — text-to-3D prompts for sim assets where the sim is 3D (target: glTF; works for Meshy, Luma Genie, TripoSR, Rodin). Listed when relevant.

Prompts are written in a "style-then-content" pattern with explicit constraints (palette, framing, what to exclude). Each is one self-contained sentence so the agent can paste it directly.

### 0.3 Visual style guide for prompts (apply across all containers)

Unless overridden in a specific cell:

- **Palette**: Clean, slightly warm whites; one bold accent (teal `#2DD4BF` or coral `#F87171`); two muted neutrals (slate, sand). No rainbow gradients.
- **Style**: Flat vector illustration with subtle grain texture, sparse line work, generous negative space; thinks "Stripe Press × Bartosz Ciechanowski blog." Avoid: photorealism, AI-glossy 3D, stock-photo people, cluttered HUDs.
- **Subjects**: Hands appear with no faces (top-down); humans drawn from behind or as silhouettes; cultural neutrality favored. Singapore-specific contexts where the concept benefits (HDB blocks for urban planning, MRT map for graph search, hawker centre for newsvendor).
- **Type**: Sans-serif for UI, slight serif for headings. Always legible at thumbnail size.
- **Motion (for INTRO video)**: 5–8 seconds, seamless loop, single concept beat, ends where it begins. No zoom-in dramatic logo reveals; no rotating Earth; no "data streams." A single elegant action.
