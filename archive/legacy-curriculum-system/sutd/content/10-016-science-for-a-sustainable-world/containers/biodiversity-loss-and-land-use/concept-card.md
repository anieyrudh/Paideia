# Biodiversity Loss and Land Use

Biodiversity loss is often driven by repeated land-use decisions: clearing,
fragmenting, restoring, or protecting habitat. Small annual changes can compound
over time, and species response is often nonlinear.

## First-Principles Explanation

The simplest model tracks intact habitat \(H\) and maps it to a biodiversity
index \(B\) with a species-area style response:

```latex
B = 100\left(\frac{H_t}{H_0}\right)^z
```

The exponent \(z\) represents sensitivity. A higher value means the same habitat
loss produces a sharper biodiversity loss. Restoration must be compared against
ongoing conversion; it is not meaningful as a standalone promise.

## Common Misconceptions

- A small annual land-use change can compound into a large long-term shift.
- Restoration is not enough if conversion pressure remains larger.
- Species sensitivity changes the size of the biodiversity response.
