# Polymers and Plastic Waste Management

## First principles

Polymer selection is a materials problem and a systems problem. A useful plastic
must have enough strength for the service, low enough mass for the function, and
a credible route after use.

For an introductory selection index, compare useful strength with mass and
embodied carbon:

```text
I = yield strength / (density * embodied carbon)
```

The index is not a final life-cycle assessment. It is a disciplined first pass
that forces the learner to ask what property is being optimised and what happens
after the item is used.

## Key definitions

- **Polymer**: a long-chain molecule whose repeat units give plastics their
  useful processing and mechanical properties.
- **Yield strength**: stress at which the material begins to deform permanently.
- **Density**: mass per unit volume; lower density can reduce material mass for
  a fixed volume.
- **Embodied carbon**: greenhouse-gas emissions associated with producing a
  unit mass of material.
- **Recovery pathway**: reuse, recycling, composting, incineration, or landfill
  route available in the local waste system.

## Common misconception

A recycling symbol or biodegradable label does not guarantee low impact. The
local collection system, contamination, reuse rate, and required performance can
dominate the decision.
