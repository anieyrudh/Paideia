# Sodium And Chlorine Valence

## Prompt

Use electron configurations to compare sodium and chlorine. Explain which atom has one outer electron, which has seven outer electrons, and how that supports an ionic bonding prediction.

## Worked Solution

Sodium has `Z = 11`, so a neutral sodium atom has 11 electrons. Filling the shells gives `1s2 2s2 2p6 3s1`, or `2, 8, 1`. The outer shell has one electron.

Chlorine has `Z = 17`, so a neutral chlorine atom has 17 electrons. Filling the shells gives `1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p5`, or `2, 8, 7`. The outer shell has seven electrons.

The same concept transfers: atomic number fixes neutral electron count, filling order gives the outer shell, and the outer-shell count explains why sodium can lose one electron while chlorine can gain one electron in an ionic bonding model.

## Rubric

- Identifies `Z = 11` for sodium and `Z = 17` for chlorine.
- States that neutral electron count equals atomic number.
- Gives correct shell summaries: sodium `2, 8, 1`; chlorine `2, 8, 7`.
- Connects one valence electron and seven valence electrons to the bonding prediction without claiming this model is a full reaction mechanism.
