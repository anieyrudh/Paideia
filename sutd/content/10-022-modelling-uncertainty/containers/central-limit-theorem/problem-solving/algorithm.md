# Central Limit Theorem Problem-Solving Algorithm

1. Identify the population variable, its mean mu, and its standard deviation sigma.
2. Confirm that the statistic is a sample mean from repeated independent samples of the same size n.
3. Set the sampling-distribution centre to mu.
4. Compute the standard error as sigma / sqrt(n), keeping the original measurement unit.
5. Decide whether the sample size and context make a normal approximation reasonable.
6. Interpret the result as a claim about repeated sample means, not about individual observations.

## Formula Standard

```latex
\mu_{\bar X} = \mu
\qquad
\sigma_{\bar X} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}
```

Legend:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mu | population mean | original observation unit |
| x-bar | sample mean | original observation unit |
| sigma | population standard deviation | original observation unit |
| n | sample size | count |
| sigma-bar | standard error of the sample mean | original observation unit |
