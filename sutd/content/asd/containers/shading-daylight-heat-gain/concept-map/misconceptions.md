# Misconception Map

## More glass always improves daylight quality

- Evidence: CIBSE LG10 frames daylight design as a balance between aperture, visual comfort, and solar control rather than maximum window area.
- Surface in predict? Yes. The first distractor says daylight and heat gain both increase, which catches learners who maximise glass or sun exposure without checking glare and heat.
- Correction: More glass raises possible daylight but also raises exposed area for direct solar gain; shading and orientation decide whether the extra aperture is useful.

## Shading only reduces light and never heat

- Evidence: Whole Building Design Guide sun-control guidance treats external shading as a solar-gain control strategy as well as a daylight and glare device.
- Surface in predict? Yes. The third distractor says only daylight changes and heat gain is controlled only by wall insulation.
- Correction: The same shaded fraction reduces the sunlit glass area in the daylight proxy and the direct solar heat-gain proxy.

## A single metric decides the facade

- Evidence: Environmental design decisions require reading daylight, glare, heat gain, view, and constructability together.
- Surface in predict? Yes. The fourth distractor says a deeper shade always improves every performance metric.
- Correction: A deeper overhang can reduce heat gain while making the daylight score too low, so the design target is a defensible tradeoff rather than a single maximum.
