# Solar Energy and Band Theory

Solar cells convert light into electrical energy only when photons can lift
electrons across a semiconductor band gap. The useful threshold is not
"brighter light always wins"; it is the comparison between photon energy and
the gap:

\[
E_{photon}=\frac{hc}{\lambda}
\]

Shorter wavelength photons carry more energy. If \(E_{photon}<E_g\), the photon
is below the gap and does not create an electron-hole pair in the simple model.
If \(E_{photon}\ge E_g\), the photon can be absorbed, but energy above the gap is
mostly thermalised as heat rather than becoming proportionally more electrical
work.

## First-Principles Explanation

The electromagnetic wave arrives as photons with energy set by wavelength.
The semiconductor provides discrete allowed bands separated by a gap. A photon
can create an electron-hole pair only when its energy is at least the gap:
the threshold is a comparison of two energies, not a statement about brightness
alone. Above threshold, the band gap sets the useful excitation energy in this
simple model, while extra photon energy is quickly dissipated to the lattice.

## Must-Know

- Band gap \(E_g\) is the minimum energy needed to move an electron from the
  valence band to the conduction band.
- Photon energy increases as wavelength decreases.
- Above-gap photons are useful only up to the band-gap energy in this first
  model; excess energy becomes thermal loss.
- Below-gap photons pass through or are not productively absorbed.

## Common Traps

- Treating all wavelengths as equally useful for one semiconductor.
- Assuming blue photons give proportionally more voltage than red photons.
- Confusing high irradiance with meeting the photon-energy threshold.
