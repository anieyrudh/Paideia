# Water Quality and Treatment

Water treatment is a chain of checks, not a single visual inspection. Clarification
and filtration reduce turbidity, disinfection reduces pathogen risk, pH affects
chemistry and acceptability, and flow conditions shape whether the process has
enough contact time.

## First-Principles Explanation

Particles, pathogens, and dissolved chemical conditions are different hazards.
Filtration can make water look clear while microbial risk remains. Disinfection
depends on the product of disinfectant concentration and contact time:

```latex
CT = C_{chlorine}t
```

The finished-water decision therefore combines physical removal, chemical
exposure, and pH banding. A safe-looking treatment train can still fail when one
of those evidence streams is missing.

## Common Misconceptions

- Clear water is not automatically safe water.
- A high disinfectant dose does not remove the need for contact time.
- pH is not cosmetic; it changes treatment chemistry and operating decisions.
