# Thermochemistry and Equilibrium

## First-Principles Explanation

Thermochemistry tracks energy transfer. If a sample warms, energy has entered that sample; if it cools, energy has left it. For a simple calorimetry model, the heat transferred to the sample is

```latex
q = mc\Delta T
```

Equilibrium tracks composition and rates. A reaction mixture can be product-rich, reactant-rich, or nearly balanced depending on concentrations and the equilibrium constant. A reaction quotient compares the current composition:

```latex
Q_c = \frac{[products]}{[reactants]}
```

Heat direction and equilibrium composition are connected by reaction energetics, but one measurement cannot replace the other.

## Why This Matters

Sustainable systems use heat and equilibrium together: batteries, carbon capture, fertiliser production, hand warmers, thermal storage, and water treatment all depend on energy changes and composition limits.

## Canonical Example

A 100 g water sample warming from 25 C to 45 C absorbs about 8.4 kJ. That tells us heat entered the water. It does not by itself tell whether a reacting mixture is product-favoured; concentration data or an equilibrium constant is needed.

## Common Misconceptions

- Exothermic reactions are not automatically product-favoured under every condition.
- Equilibrium does not mean equal amounts; it means the forward and reverse rates balance.
- A temperature probe measures energy transfer evidence, not the full reaction composition.
