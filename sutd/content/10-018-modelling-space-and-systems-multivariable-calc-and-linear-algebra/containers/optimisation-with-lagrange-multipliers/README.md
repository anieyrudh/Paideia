## What this teaches

Use gradient alignment to identify constrained optima and interpret the multiplier as a resource sensitivity.

For an unconstrained optimum, the gradient of the objective is zero. With a constraint \(g(x,y)=c\), feasible moves must stay tangent to the constraint curve. At a constrained optimum, no tangent move can improve the objective to first order.
