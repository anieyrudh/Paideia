# Forces and Equilibrium

## First-principles explanation

An object is in equilibrium when all the forces on it cancel. That does not mean
there are no forces; it means the horizontal forces sum to zero and the vertical
forces sum to zero. If either resultant is non-zero, the object accelerates in
that direction.

## Key Definitions

- **Force**: a push or pull measured in newtons.
- **Resultant force**: the vector sum of all forces acting on an object.
- **Equilibrium**: the condition where the resultant force is zero.
- **Component balance**: checking horizontal and vertical resultant forces separately.

## Why This Matters

Equilibrium is the bridge between vector addition and real mechanics. It lets
students solve support, tension, weight, and contact-force problems without
guessing which arrows cancel.

## Canonical Examples

- A box pulled left while a support force pushes right.
- A hanging object where tension and weight balance vertically.

## Common Misconceptions

- Balanced forces must be absent or invisible.
- One large force can balance perpendicular forces without components.

## What The Student Does

The student predicts the balancing support forces, adjusts rightward and upward
support forces, and reveals the net horizontal force, net vertical force, and
the formula used to decide whether the object is in equilibrium.

## Pedagogical Choices And Why

- **Predict format**: multiple choice, because the options separate horizontal
  and vertical balancing from scalar addition.
- **Manipulate variables**: only the support forces are adjustable so students
  focus on matching each component to the fixed opposing force.
- **Transfer problem**: a hanging sign uses the same zero-resultant condition in
  a new physical context.

## Misconceptions This Surfaces

- **Balanced means equal-looking arrows** — the reveal forces students to check
  `sum Fx` and `sum Fy`, not visual symmetry alone.
- **Zero net force means no forces act** — the object can have four forces and
  still have zero resultant.

## Notes For The Teacher

Ask students to write the two equilibrium equations before touching the sliders:
`sum Fx = 0` and `sum Fy = 0`.
