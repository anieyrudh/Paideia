# Scalars and Vectors

## What this teaches

Students learn that a physical quantity is not classified by its unit or by
whether its number can be positive or negative. The useful test is whether the
quantity needs direction as part of its meaning. Scalar quantities are complete
with magnitude and unit; vector quantities need magnitude and direction, so
vector addition must preserve direction instead of adding arrow lengths as
ordinary numbers.

## What the student does

- **Predict:** Choose a resultant magnitude before any vector construction is
  shown.
- **Manipulate:** In the later sim build, drag two coplanar vector arrows and
  change the angle between them.
- **Observe:** Compare the geometric resultant with the simple sum of
  magnitudes.
- **Explain:** State why the same two magnitudes can produce different
  resultants when the angle changes.
- **Transfer:** Decide whether a quantity in a mechanics question should be
  added algebraically or vectorially before calculating.

## Pedagogical choices and why

- **Why this predict format?** A multiple-choice magnitude prediction forces
  students to commit to whether direction affects resultant magnitude before
  they see the construction.
- **Why this transfer problem?** Mechanics later reuses the same distinction for
  displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, and momentum; the transfer is to
  classify the operation before computing.
- **What misconceptions does this surface?** It surfaces magnitude-only vector
  addition and the habit of treating vector signs as ordinary scalar signs.

## Notes for the teacher

The SEAB 2027 H2 Physics syllabus places scalars and vectors in Foundations of
Physics, with learning outcomes to distinguish scalar and vector quantities,
add and subtract coplanar vectors, and represent a vector as perpendicular
components. This package deliberately stays at the classification and
representation boundary; full mechanics examples should live in later
containers.
