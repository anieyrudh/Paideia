# Transfer Problem: Unit Consistency Check

A student writes a measured acceleration as `9.8 m s^-1`.

## Expected Reasoning

Acceleration is a derived vector quantity: it is change in velocity per unit
time. Velocity has unit `m s^-1`, so acceleration has unit:

```text
(m s^-1) s^-1 = m s^-2
```

The corrected record is:

```text
9.8 m s^-2
```

The dependency chain is:

```text
length + time -> velocity
velocity + time -> acceleration
```

That chain also explains why direction may matter in later vector problems:
acceleration is not just a number with a unit; it is a physical quantity whose
sign or direction describes how velocity changes.

## Rubric

- Identifies acceleration as the physical quantity.
- Distinguishes velocity units from acceleration units.
- Classifies acceleration as a derived vector quantity.
- Reduces the unit to base SI form.
- Explains why `m s^-1` is not dimensionally consistent for acceleration.
