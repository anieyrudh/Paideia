# Magnetic Fields

## First-Principles Explanation

A magnetic field describes a region where moving charge can experience a force. The force is not along the magnetic field line. For a current-carrying wire or a moving positive charge, the force is perpendicular to both the direction of motion or conventional current and the magnetic field direction.

The size of the force depends on how much of the current or velocity is perpendicular to the field. A wire of active length \(L\) carrying current \(I\) in magnetic flux density \(B\) has force \(F = BIL\sin\theta\). A particle with charge \(q\) and speed \(v\) has magnetic-force magnitude \(F = |q|vB\sin\theta\). The angle \(\theta\) is between the current or velocity direction and the magnetic field.

Direction is a separate step from magnitude. Fleming's left-hand rule gives the force direction for conventional current or a positive charge. A negative charge has the opposite force direction. If the charge is stationary, or if the motion is parallel to the field, the magnetic force is zero.

## Key Definitions

- **Magnetic flux density \(B\):** field strength measured in tesla, where one tesla gives one newton per ampere metre for a perpendicular wire.
- **Motor effect:** force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.
- **Active length \(L\):** length of conductor that lies inside the magnetic-field region.
- **Fleming's left-hand rule:** first finger field, second finger conventional current, thumb force.
- **Moving-charge force:** \(F = |q|vB\sin\theta\), with direction reversed for a negative charge.
- **Circular path in a field:** for perpendicular motion, magnetic force can supply the centripetal force, so \(r = mv/(|q|B)\).

## Canonical Examples

- A horizontal wire carrying conventional current to the right in a field into the page feels a force up the page.
- A positive particle moving to the right in a field into the page curves upward; a negative particle with the same velocity curves downward.
- A stationary charged particle in a magnetic field has no magnetic force because \(v = 0\).
- A conductor parallel to the magnetic field has no motor-effect force because \(\sin 0^\circ = 0\).

## Common Misconceptions

- **Force follows the field line:** magnetic field direction is not the direction of the magnetic force.
- **Any charge in a magnetic field feels force:** magnetic force requires motion with a component perpendicular to the field.
- **Left-hand rule ignores charge sign:** the rule follows conventional current or positive charge velocity; negative charges reverse the result.
- **Circular motion adds a new force:** the magnetic force is the centripetal resultant when it bends the path.

## A-Level Problem-Solving Pattern

1. Identify whether the object is a wire with current or a particle with charge and velocity.
2. Convert units to SI: millitesla to tesla, centimetres to metres, microcoulombs to coulombs.
3. Use the magnitude formula: \(F = BIL\sin\theta\) or \(F = |q|vB\sin\theta\).
4. Use Fleming's left-hand rule for direction, then reverse only if the moving particle is negatively charged.
5. For circular paths, equate \(Bqv\) to \(mv^2/r\) only when the velocity is perpendicular to the field.
