# Orbital Speed Check Transfer Problem

## Problem

A satellite of mass 120 kg moves at 7600 m/s in a circular orbit of radius `6.8 x 10^6 m`. Find the inward acceleration, the gravitational force required, and state how the force demand changes if the same satellite moves faster at the same orbital radius.

## Worked Solution

Formula:

```text
a_c = v^2 / r
F_c = m a_c = m v^2 / r
```

Substitution:

```text
a_c = (7600 m s^-1)^2 / (6.8 x 10^6 m)
    = 8.49 m s^-2

F_c = (120 kg)(8.49 m s^-2)
    = 1.02 x 10^3 N
```

Interpretation: the satellite needs about `1.02 x 10^3 N` of inward resultant force. In orbit, gravity supplies this inward resultant force. At the same orbital radius, increasing speed increases the required inward force by the square of the speed change.

## Rubric

- 1 mark: states that the required acceleration and resultant force are inward, toward the orbital centre.
- 1 mark: uses `a_c = v^2 / r` with SI units.
- 1 mark: obtains `8.49 m s^-2` or a correctly rounded equivalent.
- 1 mark: uses `F_c = m a_c`.
- 1 mark: obtains about `1.02 x 10^3 N` inward and identifies gravity as the real force source.
- 1 mark: states that increasing speed at fixed radius increases force demand quadratically.

## Misconception Check

Do not add a separate centripetal force in addition to gravity. In this context, gravity is the inward resultant force that produces the circular motion.
