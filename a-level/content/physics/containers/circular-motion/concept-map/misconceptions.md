# Misconceptions

## Constant Speed Means Zero Acceleration

Evidence: OpenStax University Physics Volume 1, Section 4.4 Uniform Circular Motion, explains that uniform circular motion has acceleration directed toward the centre even when speed is constant.

Surface in predict?: yes.

This is surfaced in the prediction checkpoint. The corrective move is to separate speed from velocity: speed can remain constant while velocity changes direction. The acceleration is therefore non-zero and radial.

## Centripetal Force Is An Extra New Force

Evidence: OpenStax University Physics Volume 1, Section 6.3 Centripetal Force, frames centripetal force as the net force required for circular motion.

Surface in predict?: no.

The simulation avoids adding a second force label. It labels the inward arrow as the centre-seeking resultant force, then asks learners to name the real interaction in transfer tasks.

## The Force Points Outward

Evidence: OpenStax University Physics Volume 1, Section 6.3 Centripetal Force, uses inward net force for circular motion and distinguishes it from non-inertial descriptions.

Surface in predict?: yes.

The visual keeps the velocity tangent and the acceleration/resultant-force arrow inward at every point on the circle. The transfer prompt distinguishes the outward sensation in a turning frame from the inward resultant force in an inertial analysis.
