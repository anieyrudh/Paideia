## Anieyrudh Filter pass

Date: 2026-05-21

### P0 resolved
- Prediction-before-reveal could be bypassed if the observation panel mounted directly. Resolution: mounted observe/explain through `@paideia/sim-runtime`, which wraps reveal through `@paideia/prediction-gate`, and added package plus sim-harness prediction-gate tests.
- Prediction could be contaminated by pre-commit numerical acceleration. Resolution: removed the before-reveal radial acceleration readout; learners can manipulate settings before reveal, but formula values and inward force evidence appear only after prediction commit.
- Circular-motion formulas could drift into the container or sim UI. Resolution: `a_c = v^2 / r`, `F_c = ma_c`, angular speed, and period calculations are owned by `core/mechanics`; the sim only renders geometry and calls the kernel.
- Student UI could teach centripetal force as a separate extra force. Resolution: the UI labels the arrow as the centre-seeking resultant force, and the transfer prompt asks learners to name the real force source.
- Transfer could be near-isomorphic to the sim sliders. Resolution: replaced the flat-track transfer with an orbital gravity problem at satellite scale.

### P1 addressed or deferred
- Constant speed could be mistaken for zero acceleration. Addressed with a prediction option, tangent velocity arrow, inward acceleration/resultant-force arrow, and interpretation text.
- Formula work could become detached from the visual. Addressed with nearby color legend, substitution, SI units, and readout cards adjacent to the diagram and speed-force chart.
- Radius and speed effects could be hidden in a form. Addressed with presets and live visual/readout updates before reveal, then charted force comparison after prediction commit.
- Public sim/kernel quantities could cross package boundaries as bare numbers. Addressed with branded `MetresPerSecondSquared`, `RadiansPerSecond`, `Degrees`, `Newtons`, `Seconds`, `Metres`, and `MetresPerSecond` types in public result/state interfaces.

### High-bandwidth questions surfaced
- Should future dynamics containers add a shared free-body force-source classifier for contexts such as friction, tension, and gravity?
- Should `core/mechanics` expose branded acceleration and angular-speed unit constructors if more containers need those composite quantities?
