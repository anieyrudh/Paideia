# Electric Fields

## First-Principles Explanation

An electric field describes how a source charge changes the space around it. At any point, the electric field strength is the force per unit positive test charge placed at that point. That definition separates the field made by the source from the force felt by a particular test charge.

For a point source charge, the field becomes weaker with the square of distance:

```text
E = kQ / r^2
```

The sign of the source charge fixes the field direction. Field lines point away from a positive source and towards a negative source. A positive test charge feels force in the field direction; a negative test charge feels force opposite to the field direction.

Electric potential is related but not identical. Potential is energy per charge, while field strength is force per charge. The energy link is:

```text
Delta U = q Delta V
```

This is why a potential difference can accelerate a charged particle even when the field is not drawn as a force arrow.

## Key Definitions

- **Electric field strength, E:** force per unit positive test charge at a point, measured in N/C or V/m.
- **Source charge, Q:** the charge that creates the field being studied.
- **Test charge, q:** the charge placed in the field to find the force on it.
- **Electric potential, V:** electric potential energy per unit charge, measured in volts.
- **Potential difference:** change in potential between two points; it gives energy change through Delta U = q Delta V.

## Canonical Examples

- A positive source charge makes an outward field; a positive test charge is repelled outward.
- A positive source charge still makes an outward field, but a negative test charge is attracted inward because F = qE.
- Doubling the distance from a point charge reduces field strength to one quarter.
- Moving a charge through a potential difference changes its electric potential energy by q Delta V.

## Common Misconceptions

- **Negative charges follow field arrows:** Field arrows show the force direction for a positive test charge only.
- **Voltage equals electric field strength:** Voltage is energy per charge; field strength is force per charge. They have different units and different roles.
- **Changing the test charge changes the field:** The source charge and position set E. Changing q changes F, not E.

## Why This Matters

Electric fields connect force, energy, and circuits. The same idea explains charged particle motion, capacitors, electron beams, Millikan-style oil drops, and why potential difference transfers energy per unit charge.
