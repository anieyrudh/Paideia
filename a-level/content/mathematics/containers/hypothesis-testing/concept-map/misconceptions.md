# Misconception Map

## p-value is the probability the null hypothesis is true

**Evidence:** Students often reverse the conditional statement in hypothesis testing, reading \(P(\text{data or more extreme} \mid H_0)\) as \(P(H_0 \mid \text{data})\). OpenStax Chapter 9 and the H2 Mathematics hypothesis-testing syllabus both frame p-values as evidence under an assumed null model, not posterior probabilities.

**Surface in predict?** Yes. The prediction options include "The p-value is the probability that the null hypothesis is true."

**Correction:** Say "if \(H_0\) were true, this result would be rare" rather than "\(H_0\) is probably false by this exact percentage."

## rejecting the null proves the alternative is true

**Evidence:** A-Level mark schemes require contextual decisions at a stated significance level. That wording does not license proof language, because the test still depends on modelling assumptions, tail choice, and the pre-agreed significance level.

**Surface in predict?** Yes. The prediction options include "The null hypothesis is proved false whenever the sample mean is different."

**Correction:** Rejection means the sample is sufficiently unusual under \(H_0\) to support the alternative as a decision, not as proof.

## only the observed difference matters

**Evidence:** Learners frequently compare \(\bar{x}\) with \(\mu_0\) directly and ignore \(\sigma / \sqrt{n}\). The same raw gap can cross or miss the critical region depending on spread and sample size.

**Surface in predict?** Yes. The opening prediction asks what happens when sample size grows while the observed mean stays fixed.

**Correction:** Convert the raw gap into standard errors before judging strength of evidence.
